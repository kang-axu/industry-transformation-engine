# ITE Principles

These principles are provisional and are being tested against real transformations and historical evidence.

## 1. Start with reality, not architecture
Observe the real business before designing the system.

## 2. Find giants before reinventing
Study proven people, organizations, methods, and historical analogies before building from scratch.

## 3. Calibration is foundation
Do not scale outputs before real language, facts, rules, boundaries, and acceptance criteria are calibrated.

## 4. Transform the workflow, not just the toolset
New infrastructure creates the largest gains when the process is redesigned around it.

## 5. Separate core, industry, and client layers
Over-standardization destroys fit.
Over-customization destroys scale.

## 6. Every project must leave reusable assets
A project that leaves only revenue is incomplete.

## 7. Human judgment is a system component
Do not hide expert escalation outside the architecture.

## 8. Let FTD convert failure into experience
A documented mistake is not yet learning.
Learning requires correction, validation, and behavioral update.

## 9. Migrate before your advantage becomes a commodity
Today's rare capability will become tomorrow's default feature.

## 10. Do not underwrite what you cannot control
Share upside when appropriate.
Do not absorb uncontrolled downside.

## 11. Heavy is acceptable; uncontrolled is not
Complexity is not the enemy.
Uncontrolled complexity is.

## 12. Protect predictable cash flow
Growth that can be destroyed by a few bad commitments is fragile.

## 13. The engine must become cheaper to run over time
Repeated work should increasingly become reusable methods, modules, data, and standards.

## 14. Models are replaceable; validated experience should not be
The organization must survive tool and model turnover.

## 15. Reality has final authority
Not the human.
Not the AI.
Not the framework.
